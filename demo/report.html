KETCHUP kinetic parameter estimation, wrapped as a process-bigraph Step

KETCHUP (Maranas group) fits the kinetic parameters of a metabolic network by solving one IPOPT nonlinear program so the model reproduces measured steady-state fluxes and metabolite concentrations. KetchupEstimator bridges the genuine ktools solver — its update() builds the real Pyomo model and calls IPOPT; nothing here is reimplemented or mocked.

Two capabilities are shown. Part I — steady-state: large-scale flux fitting on two K-FIT E. coli models (k-ecoli74, k-ecoli307) plus a seed-driven multistart. Part II — time-series: the dynamic KETCHUP extension (Hu, Jilani, Olson & Maranas, PLOS Comput Biol 2025) fitting cell-free enzyme kinetics (FDH, BDH) to NADH-vs-time data — reproducing the paper's Fig 2 via a second Step, KetchupDynamicEstimator.

Solves are bounded (max_iter / max_cpu_time) so the demo finishes in ~127s total — reported solutions are genuine but partial, and each run's IPOPT termination condition is shown as-is. The objective rises while primal infeasibility falls: IPOPT is driving a random initial guess onto the steady-state manifold (satisfying every rate-law constraint), trading apparent error for feasibility. Charts show only normal iterations (restoration-phase sub-iterations are excluded). Use the vendored ipopt.opt (max_iter 5000) for full convergence.
